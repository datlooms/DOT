**The overall stance has a name: severe testing.** A test is *severe* if it would very probably have exposed a flaw had one existed. That's Deborah Mayo's formulation of Popper's falsification, and it's exactly what you've been doing — not looking for confirmation, but constructing tests the system could plausibly fail.

**In quant finance the umbrella term is backtest overfitting detection**, and your project already uses some of its vocabulary — `PBO via CSCV` appears in `selection_constraint_evaluation.csv`.

## The named methods you're closest to

**PBO — Probability of Backtest Overfitting**, via CSCV (combinatorially symmetric cross-validation). Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado.

**Deflated Sharpe Ratio** — same authors. Adjusts a performance figure for how many trials produced it.

**And Harvey, Liu & Zhu, *"…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns"*** — the paper that made multiple-testing correction standard in finance. Its argument is precisely your trial-count problem: a t-stat of 2 means nothing when 300 factors were tested.
