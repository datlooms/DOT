2. THE TOKEN LIST DOES NOT CATCH THE IDIOM THE ARGUMENT IS BUILT ON. This is the finding of the pass.

Appendix D's load-bearing reason for extending the sweep is stated in its own words: "dots_thresholds._floor_pct (L54-63) is sorted_vals[int(floor(count*pct))], indexed off sorted(rings[col]) at L106. Mechanism D — the project's canonical, sacred, byte-locked threshold mechanism — IS a sorted-index percentile… a contributor writing a look-ahead threshold in that style would sail straight past the abort."

I verified both lines. L54-63 is exactly that. And L106 is srt = {col: sorted(rings[col]) for col in roll_cols} — the Python builtin sorted, not np.sort.

sorted( is not in the check's token list (np.percentile, np.quantile, .quantile(, np.sort, np.argsort, searchsorted). So the appendix cites mechanism D as proof that the sweep must cover sorted-index idioms, and then ships a sweep that does not catch mechanism D's own idiom. A contributor writing srt = sorted(vals); cut = srt[int(len(srt) * 0.9)] — the exact house style the argument names — sails past the abort just as surely as before the extension. The extension closed np.sort and left open the one form the reasoning rests on.

This is not a manufactured concern; it is the section's own stated rationale failing against its own example.

And the obvious fix is worse than the defect. Adding sorted( to the token list yields 74 additional sites across 24 files, nearly all plainly ordering-for-iteration (sorted(glob.glob(...)), sorted(set(...)), sorted(os.listdir(...))). The manifest would go from 34 to 108 and the signal would drown. Two workable routes instead:

Narrow on the indexing, not the sorting. The dangerous construct is a sorted array subscripted by a computed fraction of its own length. That is greppable with far better precision than sorted( alone — flag a subscript whose index expression contains len(, count, .size or .shape applied to the same array.
Or move to file-level locking, which is the project's own precedent. Byte-lock the modules that may legitimately define market objects — terrain.py, cluster_profiler.py, and dots_thresholds.py (already locked) — with sha manifests, exactly as the sacred five are locked. Any change to a market-object-defining file then requires re-blessing regardless of idiom, and there is no false-positive problem at all.
